Independent oracles and limits:

1. The persisted instant is May 1 at 00:05 in Kathmandu (+05:45), so its calendar
   date is May 1. The returned bucket is April 30. This proves the fractional-
   offset activity defect, not all possible streak or DST combinations.
2. Both instants belong to May 2024 in Kathmandu. The last second must belong
   to May, so the monthly count must be 2. The store returns 1. Investigation
   points to an inclusive final timestamp truncated to Unix seconds and used
   with an exclusive SQL comparison.
3. After clearing listens, the two instants belong to distinct local dates but
   the same UTC date. The civil active-day count must be 2; it returns 1. This
   case uses the real current clock only as the upper cutoff. Both 2024 instants
   are safely historical at execution on 2026-11-05; the clock is not injected
   into GetActiveDays in this diagnostic.
4. At 2024-12-31T23:30Z, Kathmandu is already January 1, 2025. The January
   selection without a year should therefore select January 2025, not 2024.
5. January 1, 2021 belongs to ISO week 53 of ISO year 2020. Its week begins on
   December 28, 2020; the helper returns January 3, 2022 instead.
